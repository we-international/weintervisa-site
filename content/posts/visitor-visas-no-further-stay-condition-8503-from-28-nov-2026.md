The Australian Government has made new regulations that make the "No Further Stay" condition (condition 8503) standard on visitor visas. Here is a neutral summary, checked on 16 November 2026.

## What is changing

- The Migration Amendment (Preventing Further Stay for Visitor Visas) Regulations 2026 were registered on 13 November 2026.
- As reported, from 28 November 2026 condition 8503 will apply to new applications for the Electronic Travel Authority (subclass 601), eVisitor (subclass 651) and Visitor visa (subclass 600).
- Condition 8503 generally means you cannot apply for another visa while you are in Australia, for example a Student visa or a further Visitor visa. Some limited applications, such as protection visas, are not blocked.
- It is reported to depend on when the visitor visa application is lodged, not when you arrive. Visitor visas applied for before 28 November 2026 are reported not to have the condition added, but some subclass 600 visas already carry it, so check your own conditions in VEVO.
- Allowed stay periods are not changing (for example, up to 3 months per visit on an ETA or eVisitor).

Official source: [Migration Amendment (Preventing Further Stay for Visitor Visas) Regulations 2026 (F2026L01503), Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01503/asmade). Details of commencement and coverage above are as reported by VisasNews and Deep Arrival (13 November 2026); always confirm on the official pages.

## Who this may affect

People planning to visit Australia first and then decide to study here. If your visitor visa has condition 8503, you would generally need to apply for a Student visa from outside Australia. Since 2 October 2026, many temporary visa holders already cannot apply for a Student visa onshore, so plan your study visa from home where possible.

Student visa and Working Holiday visa holders are not covered by this change, as reported.

## Waivers

Home Affairs can waive condition 8503 only in limited cases, generally where there has been a major change in circumstances beyond your control after the visa was granted (for example, serious illness or a natural disaster). Wanting to stay longer or deciding to study is generally not enough. A waiver request uses form 1447 and does not extend your stay.
