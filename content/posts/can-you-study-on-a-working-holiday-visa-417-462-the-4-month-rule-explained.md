If you're in Australia on a Working Holiday visa (subclass 417) or a Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462), you might be wondering whether you can use some of your time to study. The short answer: yes — but only for a limited time.

## The rule: up to 4 months of study or training

According to the Department of Home Affairs, mandatory visa condition 8548 limits Working Holiday Makers to a maximum of **4 months of study or training** during their stay in Australia. Home Affairs also explains that this applies per visa, so if you hold a second or third Working Holiday visa, you get another 4 months on each one.

Online courses you take with an overseas provider don't count toward this limit — but most people looking for an Australian qualification will study with an Australian provider, so the 4-month limit is the one to plan around.

## Why the 4-month limit matters when you choose a course

Many vocational courses run for 6 to 12 months or longer. On a Working Holiday visa, a course like that simply won't fit. When you compare options, check:

- **Total course length** — the whole study plan, including assessments, should fit within 4 months.
- **Work placement** — some courses (like Individual Support) include compulsory placement hours. Make sure placement is scheduled inside the same window.
- **Start date vs. visa expiry** — leave enough time to finish before your visa ends.
- **Delivery and timetable** — days, hours and campus locations vary by school.

## Check your own visa conditions first

Every visa is different. Before you enrol, check your current conditions for free on VEVO (Visa Entitlement Verification Online) through the official [Home Affairs website](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/already-have-a-visa/check-visa-details-and-conditions/check-conditions-online). You can read more on the [Working Holiday Maker work conditions page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/programs-subsite/Pages/working-holiday-maker/work-conditions.aspx).
