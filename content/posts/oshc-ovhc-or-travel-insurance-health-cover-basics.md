Health care in Australia can be expensive if you aren't covered. If you're on a Working Holiday visa (subclass 417 or 462), a student visa, or thinking about moving from one to the other, you'll hear three terms a lot: **OSHC**, **OVHC** and **travel insurance**. Here's what each one means, based on official government guidance.

## OSHC: Overseas Student Health Cover

OSHC is private health insurance designed for international students. According to the Australian Government's privatehealth.gov.au, student visa holders are required to hold OSHC. Home Affairs also says OSHC products commonly provide the minimum level of health insurance it recommends for overseas students.

- It's for people on a **student visa**.
- You usually need it for the whole length of your student visa.
- Cover levels vary between insurers, so compare what's included.

## OVHC: Overseas Visitors Health Cover

OVHC is private health insurance for temporary visa holders who are visiting or working in Australia, not studying. privatehealth.gov.au says visitors should consider taking out OVHC to help with medical costs. Some visas carry **condition 8501**, which means you must keep adequate health insurance for your whole stay.

Home Affairs "strongly recommends" that all visitors who aren't eligible for Medicare get private health insurance, whether or not it's a condition of their visa.

## What about travel insurance?

Many travellers buy travel insurance before they leave home. It can cover things like trip cancellation, lost luggage and some medical costs, but every policy is different. Check whether it covers medical treatment in Australia, for how long, and whether it still covers you while you work.

## Medicare and reciprocal agreements

Australia has Reciprocal Health Care Agreements with some countries. If one applies to you, you may get some medically necessary care through Medicare, but it isn't full cover. Check your eligibility on the Services Australia website.

## Before you buy any policy, check:

1. What hospital, GP and ambulance costs are covered
2. Waiting periods, especially for pre-existing conditions (privatehealth.gov.au notes that most insurers apply a 12-month waiting period)
3. When cover starts, since OVHC usually starts when you arrive in Australia
4. Whether the policy meets your visa conditions
