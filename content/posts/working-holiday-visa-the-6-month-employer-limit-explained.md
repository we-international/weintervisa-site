If you hold a Working Holiday (subclass 417) or Work and Holiday (subclass 462) visa, you've probably heard of the "6-month rule". Here's what it means, based on the Department of Home Affairs.

## The basic rule

Working Holiday Makers generally can't work for the same employer for more than 6 months without permission. This is separate from the 4-month study limit.

## The exceptions (from 1 January 2024)

Home Affairs says that from 1 January 2024 you can work for the same employer for more than 6 months **without asking permission** if you work in:

- **Plant and animal cultivation**, anywhere in Australia
- **Natural disaster recovery**, anywhere in Australia
- **Critical sectors**, anywhere in Australia: agriculture (including food processing), health services, aged care and disability services, childcare, and tourism and hospitality
- **Fishing and pearling, tree farming and felling, construction and mining** in specified postcodes in northern Australia
- **Different locations**, as long as you don't work in any one location for more than 6 months

Home Affairs says the critical-sector arrangement "applies until the Government makes further decisions". It could change, so check the official page before you rely on it.

## Asking for permission

If none of the exceptions apply, you can ask Home Affairs for permission to work longer. The two grounds are:

1. You've applied for a visa that allows ongoing full-time work and are waiting for the outcome.
2. Your work is critical to your employer, with supporting documents.
