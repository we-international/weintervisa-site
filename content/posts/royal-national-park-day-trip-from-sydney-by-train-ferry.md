Royal National Park, just south of Sydney, is one of the easiest big nature days you can do without a car. Sandstone cliffs, quiet beaches and part of a famous coastal walk — all reachable by public transport.

## Getting there without a car

- **Train + ferry: **take the train to Cronulla, then hop on the ferry to Bundeena. The crossing takes about 30 minutes, and ferries usually run hourly on the half-hour. It's often described as Australia's oldest commuter ferry.
- **Train only: **several stations sit on the edge of the park — Loftus, Heathcote, Waterfall and Otford — handy if you want to start a walk from the west or south.
- Check live timetables and fares on transportnsw.info before you go.

## What to do

- **The Coast Track: **the full track is about 26 km. For a day trip, try the Bundeena to Little Marley Beach section — about 6 km, around 3 hours return.
- **Wattamolla: **an ocean beach plus a calm lagoon — a popular picnic spot.
- **Wedding Cake Rock: **a famous white rock formation. It's unstable and fenced off, so admire it from behind the barrier.
- **Audley: **a historic boatshed where you can hire a kayak or rowboat.

## Stay safe

- Most beaches are not patrolled by lifeguards and can have strong rip currents. If in doubt, stay out.
- There's no drinking water on the walking tracks — carry plenty.
- Mobile reception is patchy. Download a map before you leave.
- Check NSW National Parks alerts for track closures or fire warnings on the day.
